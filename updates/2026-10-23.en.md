# Development Update – October 23, 2026
## Food, farming, and the path toward a coherent daily routine

**Language / Sprache:** [Deutsch](2026-10-23.md) | English  
[Back to the project overview](../README.en.md)

Project Genesis has made important progress over the recent development stages: food search and processing are better connected, larger fields have been cultivated in prepared tests, and the first conversations between two separate test agents have taken place. At the same time, spectator sessions have made it clear that successful individual actions do not yet add up to a coherent daily routine.

**The current priority is therefore not to add as many new capabilities as possible. Existing capabilities need to work together without unnecessary pauses, contradictory follow-up actions, or repeated unsuccessful journeys.**

This post summarizes completed development reports up to October 23, 2026. Results come from different tested development versions. Work still in progress at publication is not presented as a completed success.

## The vision remains unchanged

The long-term goal is a persistent Minecraft world inhabited by independent individuals with personalities, memories, interests, and relationships. They should develop their own projects and learn from experience. Communities, trade, conflict, and solitary lives should be possible, not prescribed.

Development still focuses on a single agent and its everyday behavior. The first isolated social tests do not yet constitute a functioning autonomous society.

## A natural 45-minute run with connected food preparation

One frozen development version ran for 45 minutes without code or prompt changes. Measured world time covered approximately 2.25 Minecraft days. The run used Survival on Normal difficulty with natural weather and day–night progression; Creepers remained disabled. There were no item grants, healing commands, teleports, or time-of-day changes during this run.

The agent hunted for food, collected items, and ate. It later processed carried raw chicken at its own furnace and ate two cooked portions. Beef was also cooked, with one steak remaining as a reserve. Stone gathering, furnace crafting, and reuse of the furnace for food and charcoal were directly observed. Four torches were also produced.

The agent resumed its own work after food-related interruptions, night, and danger. Toward the end, it used an existing hut for shelter. It did not die during this run and finished with 20 health and 17 hunger. It did take damage along the way, so this was not an entirely unharmed run.

**This is meaningful progress, but not proof of sustained food security.** One steak is a small reserve. A new mine section, an improved tool, or a productive field was not confirmed in this particular run. Later changes do not automatically inherit this 45-minute validation.

## Action flow: a sensible decision must survive execution

One specific problem appeared in crafting-table use: the agent chose to use its carried crafting table, but the local planner still sent it back to a known station. That station could be difficult to reach. The chosen method was lost between decision and execution.

In a later natural test, the agent placed its carried crafting table locally and crafted a hoe 1.13 seconds later—within the same task and without another model decision in between. Valid follow-up steps of an existing plan can also proceed immediately, provided fresh observation and safety checks still permit them.

This is the intended direction. Not every small step should trigger a new strategic decision. Danger, missing materials, or a genuine change of plan must still be taken into account.

The assessment remains critical: in the later test lasting roughly 30 minutes, about four minutes were spent on model requests, 13 minutes 44 seconds on confirmed shelter stays, and 4 minutes 13 seconds on observation, preparation, and transitions that could not be classified more precisely. The remaining time included actions, movement, and other recorded states. This breakdown is **not evidence that everything outside model requests was wasted**. It does show why a faster model alone cannot explain or eliminate the long pauses.

## Farming: larger yields are possible, sustained autonomous farming remains unproven

In a prepared Minecraft functional test, 32 planting positions were genuinely prepared, irrigated, and planted. In a separate harvesting phase with crop maturity explicitly prepared beforehand, 32 wheat were harvested, all positions were replanted, and ten loaves of bread were crafted. The yield was real; a complete natural growth cycle was explicitly not part of that evidence.

Starting without seeds was tested separately as well: after 21 normal harvesting attempts with no seed yield, the 22nd suitable attempt produced a seed. It was collected and planted within the same field project. No seeds were granted and no drops were forced. This demonstrates an acquisition chain, not a guaranteed yield after a particular number of plants.

Hoe crafting, soil preparation, and sowing have now also been observed during free play. However, a clear error occurred: a later attempt to gather dirt destroyed the freshly planted soil. Protection of that planted location was subsequently corrected and tested offline. Natural validation of that follow-up fix and consistently confirming the state of the field project remain outstanding.

**A large prepared field and one instance of autonomous sowing are not the same as self-chosen, continuously productive farming.** That long-term goal remains open.

## Memories, knowledge, and initial social interaction

The knowledge interface distinguishes general Minecraft facts, capabilities actually supported by the agent, and its current personal situation. Recipes and possible resource sources are made available selectively. Knowing a recipe does not mean that materials and reachable workstations are available. Retrieving an old observation does not make it current again.

Memory handling distinguishes direct observations, statements from other agents, and personal interpretations. A targeted test with 50,003 artificial events still returned six relevant entries while preserving important project, loss, and source references. This is a bounded retrieval result, not a guarantee of perfect memory selection in every situation.

In separate social tests, two agents with individual profiles and memories held conversations genuinely chosen by Claude. Offering, accepting, and actually transferring bread were additionally tested with explicitly identified local substitute decisions. The transfer mechanism therefore works under the tested conditions; spontaneous material cooperation during free play is not yet confirmed.

Codex has also been integrated as an alternative decision source and used in longer runs. The in-game decision process receives only the intended agent context and bounded knowledge queries. It does not freely read developer files or undiscovered world information. Actual actions, safety responses, and outcome checks remain the responsibility of the local Genesis controller.

## What is being worked on now

Spectator observations provide an important counterweight to passing functional tests. Frequent detours, long transitions, repeated station visits, and poorly connected actions still fall short of the intended behavior.

Alongside the destroyed farmland, another loss of intent was identified: a decision to use an existing shelter could turn into preparation for constructing a new one. A targeted offline correction exists for that issue too; it should not yet be treated as naturally validated.

Ongoing work focuses on coherent field projects, reliable use of existing shelter, and justified transitions between work, food, and rest. Personal pauses should not be eliminated. The question is whether a stay serves a clear purpose and whether the agent can resume meaningful activity afterward.

## How to interpret these results

Offline checks examine program behavior. Prepared Minecraft tests demonstrate actions under declared conditions. Natural integration runs evaluate a frozen version without assistance. Spectator sessions add a human assessment of visible behavior; documented time-of-day changes in those sessions are not evidence of natural nighttime survival.

Source code, internal work logs, credentials, and personal agent state remain private. This page publishes selected development evidence, not a finished software release or a general claim of autonomy.

**The next benchmark for Genesis is not simply “Can the agent perform an action?” It is “Can it pursue its own project sensibly over time, without the next action undoing its previous progress?”**
